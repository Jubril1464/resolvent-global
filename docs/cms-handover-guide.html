Website Content Guide
How to update the text, images, menus and contact details on your website — written for people who don’t work in software.

The four things to remember

  1. You edit the website through a private admin area — your normal website address followed by /admin.
  2. Every change goes live the moment you click Save. There is no separate “publish” step and no draft mode.
  3. You change words, pictures, lists and links. Layout, colours and page design are fixed by your developer.
  4. Some content is legally sensitive — read Section 8 before touching Proprietary Technologies or Ongoing projects.

How to use this guide

You do not need to read it cover to cover. Read Sections 1–3 once, then use Section 4 as your day-to-day map: look up the thing you want to change, and it tells you exactly where to go.

1Getting into the admin area

The admin area is a private part of your own website. Nobody can see it without a login, and it is not linked from any public page.

1.1 Signing in

  1. Open a web browser (Chrome, Edge or Safari all work).
  2. Type your website address followed by /admin — for example www.yourdomain.com/admin — and press Enter.
  3. Enter the email address and password you were given at handover.
  4. Click Login. You land on the dashboard.

Bookmark this page. You will use it every time.

1.2 If you forget your password

  1. On the login screen, click Forgot your password?
  2. Enter your email address and submit.
  3. Open the email that arrives and click the reset link.
  4. Choose a new password and sign in again.
If no email arrives

Check your spam folder first. If it is still missing, contact your developer — password emails are sent by a third-party mail service and occasionally need re-checking.

1.3 Adding or removing people

Logins live under Users in the sidebar. To give a colleague access, open Users Create New, enter their email address and a password, and save. To remove access, open that person’s user record and delete it.

Good practice

Give each person their own login rather than sharing one. If someone leaves, you then only have to delete their account. Everyone with a login can edit everything, so only add people who should be able to change the live website.

1.4 What you see when you sign in

The screen has two parts:

The sidebar (left)

Your table of contents. It has two headed groups: Collections (lists of things) and Globals (single settings pages). Clicking any name opens it.

The main panel (right)

Either a list of existing items, or the edit form for one item. Edit forms always have a Save button at the top right.

1.5 The single most important rule

Saving publishes immediately

This website has no draft or preview mode. The instant you click Save, the change is live for the public. Reload the public page in another browser tab to check your work — it will already show the new version.

Because of that: write and check your wording before you save, and if you are making a large change, copy the old text into a document first so you can put it back.

2How the system is organised

There are only three kinds of thing in the whole system. Once these click, the rest of the admin area is just detail.

2.1 Collections — lists of things

A collection is a list where every entry has the same shape. Training Courses is a collection: there are eight courses, and each one has a title, a duration, modules and so on. Adding a ninth course means adding a new entry to the list, and the website builds its card and its page automatically.

When you click a collection you get a table of the existing entries. Click any row to edit it, or click Create New at the top right to add one.

2.2 Globals — one-off settings pages

A global is a single set of fields that exists exactly once. Footer is a global: there is only one footer on the website, so there is nothing to list — clicking it takes you straight to the edit form.

2.3 The Media library — your pictures

Media is where every image on the site is stored. You upload a picture once and can then attach it wherever you like. Section 7 covers this in detail.

2.5 Two field names worth understanding

Slug

A slug is the readable part of a web address. A course with the slug process-safety lives at /training/process-safety. Slugs must be lowercase with hyphens instead of spaces, and no two items in the same collection may share one.

Changing a slug changes the page’s address

Anyone who bookmarked or linked to the old address will get a “page not found”, and the page’s search-engine history starts again. Fix a typo in a slug soon after publishing if you must, but avoid changing established ones.

Order

Most collections have an Order number that controls the sequence items appear in on the public site. Lower numbers come first.

3The six everyday tasks

Almost everything you will ever do is one of these six. Learn them, and the rest is simply knowing which collection to open — which Section 4 answers.

3.1 Change some wording

  1. Use the table in Section 4 to find which collection or global holds the text.
  2. Click it in the sidebar. For a collection, click the row you want.
  3. Click into the field, edit the text, and check your spelling.
  4. Click Save.
  5. Open the public page in another tab and refresh to confirm.

3.2 Add a new item to a list

  1. Open the collection in the sidebar — for example Projects.
  2. Before you start, open an existing entry and note how its fields are filled in. Copying the house style of an existing item is the fastest way to get a good result.
  3. Click Create New (top right).
  4. Fill in every field marked with an asterisk, working through each tab in turn.
  5. Set the Order number to place it where you want it (§3.3).
  6. Click Save. The new card, and its own page where the section has one, appear immediately.
A blank field is not always a problem

Optional fields are genuinely optional and the design handles them. Leaving a service’s image empty, for instance, shows a branded gradient panel — not a broken picture.

3.3 Change the order things appear in

Ordering is controlled by the Order number on each item, lowest first. To move an item, give it a number lower than the item you want it to sit above.

The tidiest habit is to number in tens — 10, 20, 30, 40 — because you can then insert something between two items by giving it 15 or 25, without renumbering everything else.

Two exceptions

Training Courses is ordered by Course Number, and Operating Approach Steps by Step. In both, that same number is also displayed on the website (“Course 3 of 8”, step “02”), and each value must be unique. So changing the order there also changes what visitors read — plan the whole sequence before you edit.

3.4 Add or replace a picture

  1. Open the item you want to illustrate and find its image field.
  2. To reuse a picture already on the site, click Choose from existing and pick it.
  3. To use a new picture, click Upload and select the file from your computer.
  4. Fill in the Alt text — a short factual description of what the picture shows. This is required, and it is what visually impaired visitors hear.
  5. Click Save.

To swap a picture out, remove the current one with the × on the thumbnail, then choose or upload the replacement.

3.5 Add a row to a repeating list

Bullet points, menu links, course modules and process stages are all repeating lists.

  1. Scroll to the list and click Add row at the bottom of it.
  2. Fill in the fields that appear.
  3. Drag the handle at the left of any row to move it up or down.
  4. To delete a row, open the small menu at its right and choose Remove.
  5. Click Save.
Some lists show only the first few entries

A course’s Key Areas list, for example, shows only its first five entries on the landing-page card, though the full list is used on the course page. Put the strongest points at the top.

3.6 Remove an item

  1. Open the item.
  2. Choose Delete from the controls at the top of the form and confirm.
Deleting cannot be undone

There is no recycle bin and no version history. Once an item is deleted, its content and its page are gone, and anyone holding a link to that page will see “page not found”.

Safer alternative: if you only want something out of sight for a while, copy its text into a document first, or move it to the bottom of the order — then you can restore it later. If you are unsure, ask your developer before deleting.

Each course is one entry with seven tabs. Wording on the public site follows the Category you choose: Short Course and Applied Course are presented as “Courses”, while Integrated Program entries are presented as “Programs” and given heavier visual weight. Two things to keep in step

Delivery options are stored once on the Training Page global because they are identical for all eight offerings — edit them there and every course updates.

Category names must match. The category on a course and the category on the Training Page grouping must be the same value, or the course will not appear under its heading. Both use the same dropdown, so just make sure you pick the same option in both places.

5.3 Industries and Industry Sectors

These are two separate lists that are easy to confuse:

The tabbed explorer on the home page — an icon and label on the left, a description and image on the right.

Setting Category to Ongoing switches the project page to its confidential presentation, which deliberately shows no approach pathway and no deliverables. 6Menus, footer and contact details

6.1 Navigation — the top menu

Open the Navigation global. Nav Links is a list of top-level menu items in the order they appear.

Each top-level item is one of two things, and never both:

  • A plain link. Give it a Label and an Href, and leave Children empty.
  • A dropdown. Give it a Label, leave Href empty, and add rows under Children — each with a label, a destination and an optional one-line hint.
Two rules

A dropdown heading is not itself a clickable page. If the group has a landing page, add it as the first child — for example a “Services” dropdown whose first child is “All Services”.

Only one level of nesting is supported. There is no way to put a dropdown inside a dropdown, and that is deliberate: deeper menus are hard to use on phones.

Addresses in Href should start with a slash for pages on your own site — /training, /projects — and with https:// for anywhere else. Always click the link on the live site afterwards; a typo here produces a “page not found” on every page of the website at once.

The two CTA fields at the bottom control the “Request Proposal” button. That same label and link are reused by the home-page hero and the call-to-action band, so changing them here changes all three places.

6.3 Contact Page

The Contact Page global holds two lists:

  • Contact Info Items — the details card in the hero. Each row is an icon, a label (“Email”, “Phone”) and the value.
  • Expect Steps — the “what happens next” sequence. Each row has a step, a title and a description. The step is typed as text rather than a number so that a leading zero is preserved: enter 01, not 1.

The enquiry form itself — its fields, its enquiry types, and the address enquiries are sent to — is built into the site and is not editable here.

7Images and the Media library

7.1 How it works

Every picture lives in Media. Upload it once and attach it to as many items as you like. Uploading the same photo twice creates two copies, so check whether it is already there before adding a new one.

7.2 Alt text is required

Every upload needs Alt text: a short, factual description of what the picture shows, for visitors using a screen reader and for search engines. Write “Wastewater treatment plant aeration basin”, not “image1” or “photo”. It is also what identifies the picture in the Media list, so good alt text makes pictures easy to find again later.

7.3 Practical advice

  • Images only. The library accepts image files. Videos are handled separately by your developer as fixed site files.
  • Use landscape photographs. Every image slot on the site is a wide card or panel; portrait photos will be cropped.
  • Aim for roughly 1600–2000 pixels wide and keep files under about 500 KB where you can. Very large files slow the page down for visitors.
  • Only upload pictures you have the right to use. Stock photography usually comes with licence conditions.

7.5 Deleting pictures

Check before you delete

A picture can be used in more than one place. Deleting it from the Media library removes it everywhere it appears, and the CMS will not necessarily warn you. If you no longer want a picture on a particular item, remove it from that item instead of deleting the file.

8Rules you must not break

Most of this CMS is safe to experiment with. Two parts are not, because what is published there affects your intellectual property and your confidentiality obligations. Read this section before editing either.

Never publish, anywhere on the site
  • Process routes, process flows or reaction pathways
  • Operating conditions or operating windows — temperatures, pressures, residence times, concentrations
  • Recipes, formulations or material specifications
  • Equipment design details, drawings, schematics or annotated equipment photographs
  • Laboratory or pilot results, yields or performance data
  • Commercial strategy, pricing structures, or named client information given in confidence

8.1 Proprietary Technologies

The page exists to generate qualified enquiries under NDA. That is why an entry has room for a name, a status and a single line of positioning, and nothing else — there is no long-description field to fill because there is not meant to be one.

  • Positioning must be pre-approved wording and stay at the level of “High-temperature industrial heating”.
  • IP Status uses only the two values in the dropdown. Do not seek other wording; consistency across the portfolio is a handover requirement.
  • Motifs are decorative. They illustrate a theme and must never be read, or described, as a real process.
  • Card images must be general industrial or science photography. No drawings, no schematics, no annotated equipment.
  • Do not remove the NDA wording. The confidentiality requirement is meant to appear twice — beside the portfolio and again beside the contact CTA. Both placements are in the Proprietary Technologies Page global, and both should stay filled in.
Changes to NDA and confidentiality wording

This wording sits in the CMS so that legal or IP review can update it without a software release. That is also why it should only be changed through that review: final wording is to be signed off by Resolvent Global and, where appropriate, an IP advisor.

8.2 Ongoing projects (the O-series)

Ongoing projects describe development work that is still confidential. They must not carry a Resolvent Global approach, an approach pathway, deliverables or target sectors, because any of those can disclose the IP pathway. They use the disclosure note, development focus and applications fields instead.

  • Set Category to Ongoing, which switches the page to its confidential presentation.
  • Fill in the Ongoing (Confidential) tab only.
  • Leave the whole Delivered Capability tab blank.
  • Keep the language high-level throughout — describe the problem being addressed and the areas of application, never how the work is done.

8.3 When in doubt

If you are not certain whether a sentence is safe to publish, do not publish it. Send it to whoever owns technical and IP sign-off at Resolvent Global first. Remember that saving is immediate: there is no window in which a mistake is invisible to the public.

A useful way to think about the split

The CMS owns anything that is a list of similar things or a piece of copy that changes over time. The website owns anything that is a one-off piece of design. If what you want to change would look different from everything around it, it is probably a developer job.

10Troubleshooting and glossary

10.1 Common problems

What you seeWhat to do
Save does nothing, or an error appearsA required field is empty. Look for red text — and check the other tabs, since the problem is often on a tab you are not currently looking at.
“Value must be unique”Another item already uses that slug, project reference, course number or step number. Choose a different one.
My change isn’t showing on the siteHard-refresh the public page: Ctrl+F5 on Windows, Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac. If it still looks old, confirm you actually clicked Save, and that you edited the right item — remember Services text lives on three different tabs.
A new course isn’t under its headingIts Category does not match a category on the Training Page global. Make both the same option.
Items are in the wrong orderCheck the Order numbers — lowest appears first. For courses use Course Number; for approach steps use Step.
A menu link gives “page not found”Check the Href in the Navigation global. Internal pages start with /; external links need the full https:// address.
A colour looks wrong on a service bandThe Accent Color must be a valid hex code — a hash plus six characters. Copy the format from another service.
A picture looks stretched or badly croppedUse a landscape image around 1600–2000 pixels wide. Portrait photos get cropped to fit wide cards.
I deleted something by mistakeContact your developer straight away — there is no undo in the CMS, and the sooner it is raised the better the chance of recovering it from a backup.
I can’t log inUse “Forgot your password?”. If the reset email never arrives, contact your developer.

10.2 Before you click Save — a quick checklist

  • Have I read the text back for spelling and tone?
  • Are all required fields filled in, on every tab?
  • If I added a picture, does it have proper alt text?
  • Is the Order number where I want this item to sit?
  • If this touches Proprietary Technologies or an Ongoing project — does it stay within Section 8?
  • Am I ready for this to be public in the next second? Because it will be.
